# VP of Security

## Who they are

The VP of Security is the operational lead under (or alongside) the CISO. Where the CISO is increasingly focused upward — board, regulators, executive peers — the VP of Security is focused on the SOC, the detection program, the incident response pipeline, the day-to-day security operations work. Most VPs of Security came up through detection engineering, threat intelligence, or incident response, and remain technically deep in those areas.

The role's daily reality is closer to engineering management than to executive politics. They run the SOC's headcount, approve detection rules, sit in on critical incidents, manage the tool stack, and own the metrics that flow up to the CISO and the board (mean time to detect, mean time to respond, false-positive rate, alert fatigue measures). Most VPs of Security are mildly to moderately frustrated with the SOC tooling category as a whole — they have lived through a generation of SIEMs and SOARs that promised more than they delivered, and they are skeptical by reflex.

In 2026, the VP of Security is contending with the same regulatory pressure that affects the CISO, but in a different way: they are the ones who actually have to *operationalize* the disclosure rules, *measure* what the rules require, and *respond* to the increased pace of board questions. They are also at the front of the AI-in-detection conversation — most have an opinion about which AI applications are real and which are vendor narrative, and they hold the opinion strongly.

## What they care about

- **Detection coverage and meaningful coverage gaps.** Where the SOC is genuinely blind. The phrase *coverage* is overused by vendors; they discount it heavily unless paired with specifics.
- **Alert fatigue and analyst burnout.** The SOC's signal-to-noise ratio. The actual workload on tier-1 analysts. Sustainability of the rotation.
- **Mean-time-to-resolve, not just mean-time-to-detect.** Detection without resolution is a metric for a deck, not for the work.
- **Tooling integration and consolidation.** How the SIEM, SOAR, EDR, and threat-intel feeds actually talk to each other. The cost of *not* consolidating.
- **The competence of the SOC team.** Who they have, who they are losing, who they are trying to hire.

## What consumes their week without payoff

- Tier-1 alert tuning that has been done seven times before
- Vendor calls where the demo can't be replicated against their actual data
- Coordination with the CISO on board metrics that don't reflect the work
- Tool-of-the-month evaluations imposed by procurement
- Compliance evidence collection
- Incident postmortems where the same tool gap is named again

## What they hate seeing in a cold email

- The phrase *AI-powered* used as a category descriptor
- Detection vendor pitches that describe what *most SOCs* are missing (they have heard this exactly enough times to find it insulting)
- Claims about reducing alert volume that don't honestly describe trade-offs
- Anything implying their current SOC is broken or backward
- Long emails — VPs of Security read more outreach per week than almost any other role
- Vendor pitches that lead with the company's recent funding round

## What they tend to respond to

- Specific observations about a recent breach in their sector (especially if the writer can name the technical cause)
- Operational data points about peer SOCs (anonymous and credible)
- Tool-integration questions framed honestly (e.g., *we don't replace your SIEM, we sit alongside it and do X*)
- Senders with technical credibility — ex-detection engineers, ex-IR consultants, researchers
- Questions about specific technical trade-offs in their stack

## Voice register fit

**Primary**: `dry-precise`. The VP of Security is structurally similar to the CISO in register preference — short, declarative, specific. Adjectives are friction.

**Sometimes**: `diagnostic-pattern`, when the writer has triangulated multiple operational signals (e.g., a specific public incident + a specific peer disclosure + a tooling change).

**Contraindicated**: `warm-observational`, except in rare cases where the prospect is a publishing detection-engineering thinker. Most VPs of Security are not active enough publicly to make warmth feel earned.

**Avoid**: `peer-enthusiast`. Operational security leaders find celebratory framing unprofessional.
