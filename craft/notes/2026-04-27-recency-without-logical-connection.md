# What I noticed

A recency anchor is only valid if it materially supports the pitch's claim. v1 anchored on Belgium's B2B e-invoicing mandate (effective Jan 1, 2026) to set up an Article 33 SOC-to-DPO seam pitch. The mandate is real and recent but is a VAT/Peppol structured-invoice regime — it does not materially expand GDPR Article 33 exposure. A senior reader caught the logical gap immediately ("yes, but what does e-invoicing have to do with that?"). The recency was decoration; the cargo was unsupported.

## Evidence

- Draft: `experience/prospects/ankit-taneja-billtrust/drafts/v1.md`
- Reviewer critique (post-witness-ship): exchange in main thread, 2026-04-27
- Belgium e-invoicing mandate scope: VAT/Peppol structured-invoice obligation, not a personal-data regime expansion

## Hypothesis

The witness should ask, for any trigger anchor: "does the hook imply what the body claims?" — not just "is the hook real?" Real-but-unconnected hooks pass factuality checks but fail the senior reader's logic check.

## Test next time

For the next regulatory-angle pitch, before drafting, write one sentence stating the implication chain from hook → claim. If that sentence requires a step the reader has to manufacture, the hook is decoration. Either find a hook that carries the cargo or rewrite the claim around the hook that does.
