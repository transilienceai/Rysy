## Trigger event

**Primary trigger (strongest):** Padlet Arcade's public beta launch in early 2026. Padlet has shipped an AI-powered game-creation platform for classrooms that — by design — runs with no student login required. This is a deliberate privacy choice (low friction for schools) but it also creates an AI compliance disclosure surface. Schools that deploy Arcade tools will need to answer: what data passes to Arcade's AI models? What is the disclosure obligation? How does this interact with district DPAs already in place for Padlet core?

Arcade is a *separate product at a separate domain* (arcade.padlet.com). This means a district with a Padlet DPA may not have an Arcade-specific DPA. This is exactly the kind of gap that district procurement officers and CTOs surface during renewal conversations.

**Why this trigger:** This is a product-side trigger (new AI product launch with associated governance questions) that connects directly to Transilience's value prop. It is recent (within last 90 days), specific to Padlet, and Nitesh is the person who would own the answer to "what is our compliance posture for Arcade?"

**Secondary trigger (regulatory clock):** The patchwork of state student data privacy laws is compounding in 2025-2026. Illinois SOPPA, California SOPIPA, New York Ed Law 2-d, Connecticut PA 16-189, and new AI-specific disclosure requirements are all active. Each new state law creates a district-by-district DPA update cycle. At 63-80 employees, Padlet has no dedicated legal/compliance function visible. This is the backdrop that makes the primary trigger credible — it's not just one compliance event, it's an accelerating pile.

**Trigger framing for email:** The Arcade launch is the sharper hook. "You just shipped a classroom AI platform with no student login. That's the right call for adoption — and it creates a DPA question you haven't had to answer before. How many districts are already asking?"
