# Witness verdict -- mike-mccune-coalesce

**Verdict**: ship

## What works

The opener earns its specificity by naming two acquisitions, the thirteen-month timeline, and the DevOps EMEA hire with SOC 2 adherence as a stated requirement -- all public, all verifiable, all connected into a structural observation that Mike will recognize as his own situation without feeling decoded or surveilled. The cost frame in P2 ("every sprint hour spent assembling SOC 2 evidence is a sprint hour not spent on the integration in flight") speaks directly to an engineering economist who tracks tooling cost-per-unit-of-output and will do the math himself without needing the number spelled out. The CTA asks a genuine operational question about workload distribution that he could answer in one line or take to a call -- proportional to what the email earned.

## What fails

The three-part parenthetical in P2 ("declared baseline, continuous drift detection, remediation human-in-the-loop") is the weakest element. It is the one construction in the email that pattern-matches to product-sheet cadence -- three parallel items in an em-dash-bounded clause. It is compressed enough to avoid reading as a pitch deck, and the rest of the email's structure is sufficiently varied that the whole-email shape does not feel templated. But it is the sentence a cynical reader's eye would catch as "vendor language," and for a reader as hype-sensitive as Mike, it is the closest the email comes to losing him. Not enough to block shipment; worth noting for the writer's calibration on future drafts.
