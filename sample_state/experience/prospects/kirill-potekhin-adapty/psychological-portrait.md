# Psychological portrait — Kirill Potekhin

**Lead ID:** kirill-potekhin-adapty
**Company:** Adapty.io
**Date:** 2026-04-30

---

Kirill Potekhin is a builder-CTO in the truest sense — a technical co-founder six years into a company he started from a problem he personally coded the first solution to (a PHP receipt validator for Apple and Google, still on his GitHub). He is in the established phase of his founder arc: Adapty is profitable at roughly $7M ARR, the team is around 120 people, and the product has crossed from single-product subscription analytics into a second product (web paywalls via FunnelFox). He is not building his reputation; he has one, within the mobile subscription infrastructure niche. His LinkedIn presence is sparse but authored — when he posts, it is because something real happened (an AWS outage, a product launch, a data finding worth sharing). He presents as CPO in product contexts and CTO in infrastructure contexts, which tells you he still straddles both roles and has not delegated either. His public voice is technical, data-anchored, and corrective — he measures vendor claims against operational numbers and publishes the difference.

What Kirill cares about, specifically and right now, is the reliability and trust surface of a payment infrastructure company that just widened its perimeter. Adapty launched web paywalls on May 12, 2025 — twelve days after the Apple antitrust ruling — integrating six PSPs (Stripe, PayPal, Paddle, Braintree, Adyen, Solidgate) that Adapty had never routed payments through before. This is the most consequential architectural decision the company has made since founding: it moves Adapty from read-path payment data (subscription receipt validation) to write-path payment data (web payment processing across multiple PSPs). He authored a substantive infrastructure post in October 2025 whose central thesis was that Adapty's reliability is not a feature but the entire point of the product — and then he gave a talk in December 2025 on the CDN architecture and three-stage fallback system that backs that claim. He is not theorizing about infrastructure reliability; he is publicly staking his professional identity on it. The convergence of the PSP expansion, the reliability doctrine he has published, and the Infrastructure Team Lead role he is actively hiring for tells you that the integrity of this expanded surface is occupying a meaningful share of his attention.

His attention right now is split across three fronts. First, FunnelFox is in post-launch scale mode — the product is live, customers are using it, and the operational burden of six PSP integrations (each with its own data flow, key rotation cadence, and compliance requirements) is real and growing. Second, he is hiring an Infrastructure Team Lead, which signals that the current infrastructure org is under-leadered for the expanded scope — he knows this and is trying to fix it. Third, he is maintaining his conference presence (Business of Apps NYC 2026 is upcoming), which means he is simultaneously in public-facing founder mode. There is no CISO at Adapty. Kirill carries the compliance posture personally — SOC 2 is claimed on the homepage but not publicly documented to Type II depth, no trust center exists, and PCI scope for the new PSP integrations is an open question. He is the person who would feel the weight of a compliance gap, and the person who would have to close it.

An email to Kirill should be warm-observational in register and short — five sentences maximum, probably four. He rewards specificity and evidence of having done the reading. The opener should anchor on a verifiable operational fact, not a compliment. The strongest anchor is the web paywall launch and its PSP expansion: it is recent, it is his decision, it materially changed Adapty's security surface, and it connects to the infrastructure-reliability doctrine he has already published. The angle should synthesize the two: his published commitment that reliability is the whole point, combined with the concrete reality that six new PSP integrations created a compliance and control-plane surface that did not exist twelve months ago. The CTA should be low-commitment and specific — a question about how they are thinking about the expanded surface, not a meeting request. On the verbatim quote question: paraphrase tightly rather than quote directly. He is a sparse poster; quoting his exact words from a blog post could read as surveillance rather than attentive reading. But operating near his language — using the frame that infrastructure *is* the product — signals that the writer has read his work without performing the reading back at him. This distinction matters for a founder who publishes infrequently and deliberately.

What will not work: any framing that lectures him on PCI scope, tokenization, or payment security basics — he built a receipt validator by hand and has been in payment infrastructure for six years. Any assumption about his team's geographic distribution or origin story that projects a narrative he has not told himself. Any founder-to-founder energy or first-name-exclamation opening. Any email longer than six sentences. Any use of "AI" as a decorative feature rather than a concrete mechanism. Any pitch that implies he has not already thought about the reliability of his own infrastructure — his October 2025 post is proof he has. The email must meet him where he already is (thinking about the expanded surface) rather than where a generic CTO persona would be. He is not a generic CTO; he is a builder-founder who personally owns both the product direction and the infrastructure trust of a profitable payment company that just made its biggest architectural bet.
