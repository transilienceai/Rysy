# Psychological portrait — Vitaly Davydov

**Lead ID:** vitaly-davydov-adapty
**Portrait date:** 2026-04-30
**Sources:** research-notes.md, campaign brief (provided inline), no persona file available

---

Vitaly Davydov is six years into building Adapty and has completed the transition from technical founder to commercial leader. His MIPT data science degree, his UCL/Turing research stint, and his Poteha Labs ML consulting work are all real — he wrote GPU tutorials and NLP Docker images — but he has not published technical content since 2022, and his GitHub is dormant. His public identity now lives entirely in the commercial-narrative layer: product launches, market data releases, founder origin stories, podcast hosting. He posts frequently on LinkedIn (424 posts, 4,479 followers) and his writing voice is storytelling-oriented, not analytical. He frames himself as someone who earned everything through execution — five YC rejections, $500 in savings, no slide deck — and he maintains a bootstrapped ethos even after raising $2.5M. His LinkedIn presence is authored and intentional, not templated. He is not performing CEO; he is performing founder-who-built-it-himself, which is a different posture and a more specific one.

What Vitaly cares about, specifically and recently, is the competitive concentration problem in subscription apps. His 2026 State of In-App Subscriptions foreword — the most prominent public statement he has made this year — frames the market in zero-sum terms: 95% of revenue goes to the top 10%, newer apps earn 25% less at the median, breaking in is getting harder. His strategic response to this is product expansion on three simultaneous fronts: FunnelFox web paywalls (live since May 2025, six PSP integrations), Adapty Mail AI lifecycle email agent (beta April 2026), and a commercial upscale via VP of Sales and Head of Growth hires. He is also the person who listens to top customers and productizes what they are already doing — his TechCrunch FunnelFox quote is the clearest evidence of his decision-making process. He does not invent from theory; he observes emergent customer behavior and builds infrastructure around it. This operating mode matters for how an email reaches him: he respects pattern recognition grounded in evidence, not speculative pitches about problems he has not encountered yet.

His attention right now is split across the three-front expansion and an active conference season. He hosted an App Leaders Talk in Istanbul in April 2026. He speaks at MAU Vegas May 19-21. He is launching Adapty Mail publicly. He is hiring a Head of Growth. He posted a contrarian take on AI failures ("2026-2027 will bring the first wave of AI failures — companies that adopted too fast, in the wrong way"), which reveals that he is not a hype-follower despite launching an AI product himself — he distinguishes between tooling that changes unit economics and tooling adopted for optics. His attention budget is that of a CEO running simultaneous product launches, commercial hires, and conference appearances in a three-week window. Anything that reaches him has to be immediately legible, commercially relevant, and short. He is not ignoring his inbox — he is triaging it at speed.

The email that lands with Vitaly is one that speaks to him as the commercial closer, not as a technical buyer or a security practitioner. He does not have a security team. Security questionnaires reach him (or now his VP of Sales) because enterprise prospects send them during deal cycles, not because he goes looking for security tooling. The right shape is: open with something that proves you read his work — his three-variable framing from the 2026 foreword is the strongest anchor because it is recent, public, and in his own voice — then extend it into the deal-cycle territory he is entering. The register should be warm-observational, not dry-precise. Vitaly writes as a storyteller and engages with narrative framing; a dry-precise email would read as enterprise sales boilerplate to him, which is the opposite of his self-identity. Length must be five sentences or fewer — he is in a high-throughput operational window and will not read a sixth sentence. The CTA should be time-anchored (MAU Vegas is three weeks out) and low-commitment — a question, not a meeting request. He responds to people who have done the reading, not people who have done the pitching.

What Vitaly would not respond to: any framing that treats security as an infrastructure concern (that is Kirill's domain and language, not his); any pitch that positions Transilience as a security-team purchase (he does not have a security team and would route it nowhere); any founder-to-founder cosplay that opens with his first name and an exclamation mark; any projection about his Russian-diaspora background or family connections; any echo of the "infrastructure as product" framing that belongs to Kirill or the execution-layer framing that belongs to Gregory. He would also reject anything that sounds like naive AI enthusiasm — his April 2026 post on AI failures shows he is actively filtering for that. An email that treats AI as self-evidently good would confirm that the sender has not read anything he has written.
