# What I noticed

When the prospect's own voice is dark — no posts, no quoted comments, no public surface — but their boss has a public position on a topic that touches the prospect's function, the boss's public narrative is often the right anchor for the pitch. Research surfaced that Ankur Ahuja (Billtrust CISO, Ankit's manager) had been publicly quoted on AI data governance: "AI doesn't get special treatment. It's the same strong audited controls that protect all our financial data." I noticed this in research notes and did not lift it into the draft, anchoring instead on a regulatory recency hook that did not carry. The boss's public position is a boundary condition the prospect has to operationalize day-to-day; pitching to that boundary is more grounded than pitching to a calendar event.

## Evidence

- Research notes: `experience/prospects/ankit-taneja-billtrust/research-notes.md` (Ahuja AI-governance quote captured)
- v1 draft did not use this signal: `experience/prospects/ankit-taneja-billtrust/drafts/v1.md`
- Reviewer critique identified Collections Agentic Procedures (Nov 2025) as the right hook — which is exactly the operational expression of Ahuja's public AI-governance position

## Hypothesis

For dark-voice prospects whose boss has a public narrative, the order of preference for anchors is: (1) the boss's public position translated into the prospect's operational reality, (2) a recent company-level operational change the prospect has to instrument, (3) a sector-level trigger event. v1's anchor was tier 3 when tier 1 was available.

## Test next time

When researcher returns a portrait with thin prospect voice but a quoted manager, profiler should explicitly recommend the manager-position-as-anchor angle and Vendy should evaluate it before defaulting to a regulatory-recency hook. Track whether emails anchored on boss-position outperform emails anchored on regulatory recency for dark-voice prospects.
