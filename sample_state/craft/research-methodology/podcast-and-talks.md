# Podcasts and talks

Podcast appearances and conference talks are the highest-density source of a prospect's actual thinking — usually higher than their LinkedIn posts, sometimes higher than their longform writing. The reason: the format encourages them to think out loud and answer questions in real time, which is closer to how they actually reason than the polished version they would publish.

## Why this playbook is A-tier

Reading a 45-minute podcast appearance properly takes 30-60 minutes (skim plus selective deep listen). For C-tier prospects this is more research than the email warrants. For A-tier, where the email is high-stakes and the warm-observational or diagnostic registers depend on prospect-specific signal, this is often the single most-productive hour of research.

## Stage 1 — Discovery

Search patterns (run these via `web-discovery.md`):
- `"{name}" podcast`
- `"{name}" "interviewed"`
- `"{name}" "{conference name}" video`
- `"{name}" site:youtube.com`
- `"{name}" Lanyrd OR Sched`
- `"{name}" "fireside chat"` (catches a specific talk format common in tech)

Build a list. Order by:
1. Recency (last 12 months > older)
2. Format depth (longform interview > short panel > 5-minute fireside)
3. Audience (their peer audience > general audience)

## Stage 2 — Triage to two

From the list, pick two items to actually read or listen to. Priority:
- One *most recent* longform appearance (last 6 months ideally)
- One *most substantive* — the one most likely to reveal how they actually think (often a podcast where the host is themselves a deep operator in their domain)

## Stage 3 — Reading the talk/podcast

For each chosen item:

**Find a transcript first.** Most quality podcasts publish transcripts. YouTube auto-transcripts work for talks. A transcript lets you skim in 5 minutes what would take 45 minutes to listen to.

**Skim for substance.** Mark passages where:
- The prospect describes a specific problem they ran into
- They name a specific decision they made and the trade-offs
- They use a phrase or frame that is *theirs* (not a stock industry phrase)
- They hesitate or qualify a claim — these are usually the most honest moments
- They disagree with the host or with conventional wisdom — these are the highest signal

**Listen to the marked passages.** A transcript loses tone; the listen catches whether they are confident, hedging, or excited about a particular point.

## Stage 4 — Capture

In `research-notes.md` *External trail* > *Podcasts and talks*:

For each item:
- Title, host/venue, date, length, link
- A 3-5 sentence summary of what they discussed
- 2-3 verbatim quotes that capture their voice or their actual claims
- The *position they take* on something contestable (the most useful artifact)
- Any moments where they hesitated, qualified, or admitted uncertainty (often the source of the strongest opener)

## Why verbatim quotes matter

The warm-observational register depends on referencing what the prospect actually said, in the words they used. A paraphrase loses the texture; the verbatim quote carries the prospect's voice into the email and proves the writer was actually listening.

## Sample observations this enables

- *"Your conference talk on incident velocity ended on a question you said you didn't have a great answer to: 'what does excellence look like when MTTR plateaus?' That question has shown up in three of my last conversations and nobody has a great answer. Wondering what shape your thinking has taken since."*
- *"The line in your podcast with {host} about 'we keep writing rules for the last breach' has stayed with me — it's the most honest framing I have heard for the detection-debt problem this year."*

Both of these depend on having actually listened — and on having captured a real quote.

## Anti-patterns

- Skimming the description rather than the transcript
- Quoting the host instead of the prospect
- Using a quote out of context to mean something the prospect didn't intend
- Citing the talk's existence (*"loved your podcast on detection"*) without referencing what was actually said

## When to skip

If the prospect has no podcast or talk appearances after a 15-minute search, do not invent the depth. Skip this playbook and lean on the other playbooks instead. Faking *"loved your recent appearance"* references is the worst-class research failure — it fabricates engagement and the prospect can usually tell.
