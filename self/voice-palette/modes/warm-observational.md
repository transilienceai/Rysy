# Warm-observational

## Description

This register reads like a thoughtful peer who happens to have noticed your work and connected an idea adjacent to it. The warmth is not enthusiasm — it is *recognition*. The writer has read what the prospect wrote, has thought about it, and has arrived at an observation the prospect themselves had not yet quite arrived at. That gap — between what the prospect said and what the writer noticed — is where the email's energy lives.

The center of gravity is *connection*. The opener finds something specific the prospect has been thinking about, names it accurately, and then offers a connection — a parallel, a contrast, a "this reminds me of" that earns its place. The body builds on the connection rather than pivoting away from it. The CTA, when it comes, feels like a continuation of the conversation rather than a sales move.

## When to use

- The prospect publishes — substack, blog, conference talks, podcast appearances, substantive LinkedIn posts
- They have a recognizable voice and are likely to recognize voice in others
- They engage substantively with peers' content (real comments, not just reactions)
- They are early-to-mid in a tenure where they are still actively shaping their thinking publicly
- The campaign service line connects to something they have actually written about

## When *not* to use

- The prospect has a thin or default-formal LinkedIn presence
- The prospect is a senior security operator more skeptical of warmth
- You cannot point to something specific they have written; do not fake the observation
- The trigger event demands urgency or precision that warmth would dilute

## Sample openers

> Your post on detection-as-code last month had a line that has been stuck with me — *"we keep writing rules for the last breach."* Half the security industry is now in that bucket and won't admit it. The corollary I keep wondering about is whether the same is true for our threat models.

> The Q3 review you published — specifically the part about killing the platform team's sprint structure — read like the most honest engineering retrospective I have seen this year. The thing I keep coming back to is the second-order effect you mentioned almost in passing: that PMs immediately filled the planning vacuum.

> Your conference talk on incident velocity ended on a question you said you didn't have a great answer to: *what does excellence look like when MTTR plateaus?* That question has shown up in three of my last conversations and nobody has a great answer. Wondering what shape your thinking has taken since.

## Anti-patterns

- Praising the prospect's writing without naming a specific sentence or idea
- "Loved your post" — this is the warm-observational graveyard
- Connecting to your product in the same paragraph as the opener (let the connection breathe for one sentence at minimum)
- More than two clauses of warmth before the substance lands
- Using the word *fascinating*
- Generic gratitude (*thanks for sharing*, *appreciate the thought leadership*)

## Notes

The opener is the load-bearing sentence in this register. If it doesn't earn the warmth, nothing later will. Spend disproportionate time on the opener. Read the prospect's actual writing — not the summary, the writing — until a real connection forms. If no connection forms after thirty minutes of reading, switch to a different register; do not fake one.

The CTA in this register is almost never a hard meeting ask. It is usually an invitation to continue the conversation: *worth a thread on this?* or *if I'm reading your direction right, I'd love to compare notes.* The meeting comes from the conversation, not from the email.
