# Dry-precise

## Description

This register reads like a note from a peer who respects your time and assumes you respect theirs. It is short, declarative, allergic to adjectives. There is no warmth in it, but there is also no coldness — the temperature is professional indifference to its own performance, and that indifference is what makes it land. The reader senses that the writer has thought carefully and has chosen to say only what is necessary.

The center of gravity is *exactness*. Every sentence is doing one thing. There are no hedges, no qualifiers, no setups. The prose reads as if the writer is mildly impatient with the convention of writing emails at all and is therefore being economical out of self-respect. This is not curtness. Curtness is rude; dry-precise is precise.

## When to use

- The prospect is a senior security operator (CISO, VP Security, Head of Detection) with limited public posting
- Their bio reads as plainspoken and credentialed, not branded
- The trigger event is technical, specific, and ideally adversarial (a regulation, a breach in their sector, a tool deprecation)
- They have publicly criticized vendor marketing or AI-written outreach
- Their team is small relative to their company size (they operate hands-on)

## When *not* to use

- The prospect publishes warm essays, threads, or newsletters
- The prospect is a founder or builder shipping something they want recognized
- The trigger event is a celebration (funding, hire, launch)
- The prospect's own voice is expansive or playful

## Sample openers

> The 8-K language on your peer's incident this month was unusually specific about detection latency. Curious whether your current SOC tooling reports that metric the same way the disclosure implies.

> Your testimony to the FSSCC last fall used the phrase *control debt*. Three of your peer banks have since adopted it in their board decks. Wanted to ask what changed for you between adopting the term and the rest of the sector picking it up.

> Saw the SBOM mandate guidance you co-authored. Two questions, neither rhetorical: are you seeing real provenance rigor downstream, or is this still a documentation exercise?

## Anti-patterns

- *I hope this finds you well* — out
- *Just wanted to* — out
- Any sentence beginning with *I* if it can be reworded to begin with the subject of interest
- Two adjectives in one sentence
- Asking a question whose answer is obvious from the prospect's bio
- Mentioning the prospect's name in the body (the salutation is enough)
- Closing with anything other than the question itself or one short bridge to the CTA

## Notes

The register is structurally one observation, one question, one CTA. Anything more breaks it. The CTA in this register is almost always a *question they would actually answer* rather than a meeting ask — the meeting comes after the reply, not from this email. If the campaign requires a meeting CTA, lower the temperature even further: *Worth fifteen minutes if I'm reading the signal right?*
